Nouns & Articles (A / An)

When we talk about our jobs or everyday objects, we must use A or An. We also need to know how to talk about more than one thing (Plurals).

What is a Noun? 🧠

A noun is a word that names a Person (doctor, Maria), a Place (hospital, Colombia), a Thing (computer, phone), or an Animal (dog, cat). Basically, it's everything around you!

Rule 1: A vs. An (Singular)

A Consonant Sound

Use "A" before a word that starts with a consonant sound (b, c, d, f, t, etc.).

Example: I am a teacher.

Example: It is a book.

An Vowel Sound

Use "An" before a word that starts with a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u).

Example: I am an engineer.

Example: It is an apple.

3. Consonant + y: -ies

City ➔ Cities

Baby ➔ Babies

4. Irregulars ⚠️

Man ➔ Men

Child ➔ Children

Person ➔ People

The Verb To Be

We use the verb To Be (am, is, are) to talk about names, ages, professions, and locations. It is the engine of English!

Subject Pronouns

🙋‍♂️ I (Yo)
👉 You (Tú / Uds)
👨 He (Él)
👩 She (Ella)
📱 It (Cosa/Animal)
👨‍👩‍👦 We (Nosotros)
🧑‍🤝‍🧑 They (Ellos)

Functions of "To Be"

We use "To Be" to describe people and things. Here are the 5 main functions:

  • 1

    Name: I am Diego.

  • 2

    Age: She is 20 years old.

  • 3

    Profession: He is a doctor.

  • 4

    Origin: They are from Mexico.

  • 5

    Description: We are happy.

⚠️ In English, we do NOT "have" years. We "are" years old!
